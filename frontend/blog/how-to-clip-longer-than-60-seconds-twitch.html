How to Clip Longer Than 60 Seconds on Twitch

Published Nov 2026 • 5 min read • By TwitchCutter Team

If you have ever tried clipping an intense CS2 overtime clutch, a hilarious 5-minute story, or a lengthy roleplay scene on Twitch, you have inevitably slammed into Twitch's biggest restriction: the 60-second clip cap.

Twitch's native clipping button (Alt + X) only allows users to capture between 5 and 60 seconds of video. For complex gaming plays, creator banter, and esports highlights, 60 seconds is nowhere near enough time.

Why Does Twitch Have a 60-Second Limit?

Twitch introduced the 60-second limit to optimize server bandwidth and encourage quick virality across social feeds. While 60 seconds works fine for single funny jokes, it actively hinders video editors, TikTok content creators, and tournament archivists who need multi-minute footage.

The Solution: Clip from the VOD Using TwitchCutter

You do not need to settle for 60 seconds, nor do you need to download a 30 GB full broadcast file. By using TwitchCutter, you can extract any length clip directly from the broadcaster's past broadcast (VOD).

Step-by-Step: Cutting Long Clips with TwitchCutter

  1. Find the Stream VOD: Go to the creator's Twitch channel, click on the Videos tab, and open the past broadcast where the moment occurred.
  2. Copy the VOD URL: Copy the URL from your address bar (e.g. https://www.twitch.tv/videos/123456789).
  3. Open TwitchCutter: Navigate to TwitchCutter Homepage and paste the link into the search box.
  4. Set Exact Timestamps: Use the interactive player and dual range scrubber to set your start time and end time. You can choose a 2-minute, 5-minute, or even 45-minute span!
  5. Click Cut & Download: Choose your quality (up to 1080p60) and hit Cut & Download Clip. Our server slices only that segment and downloads a watermark-free MP4.
